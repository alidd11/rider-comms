# UK route catalogue research

Last reviewed: 2026-09-19

This document is the editorial evidence register for Rider Comms' curated UK route catalogue for motorcyclists and driving enthusiasts. It deliberately contains no UI or design direction. Route presentation is owned separately; this file records why a road is admitted, what was rejected or deferred, and where the route facts came from.

## Admission standard

A route should only ship in the curated catalogue when all of the following are true:

1. **The ride itself is evidenced.** Prefer a motorcycle-specific editorial source with named start/end points and road numbers, or an official tourism/road authority route with enough detail to reproduce it.
2. **The route is reproducible.** Start, finish and ordered waypoints must keep navigation on the intended public-road corridor. Private/toll/off-road variants are not silently substituted.
3. **The recommendation is current enough to be responsible.** Known long-term closures, landslips or exceptional access constraints must be checked against the relevant road authority before admission.
4. **The route adds something distinct.** The catalogue should cover short local rides, half-day roads and destination rides across the UK rather than repeat the same kind of mountain pass.
5. **Safety copy is route-specific.** Generic "ride safely" text is not enough. Exposure, steep gradients, livestock, single-track sections, seasonal traffic, sparse fuel or other material planning factors are recorded where supported.
6. **Media provenance is complete.** Every photograph must have an exact Wikimedia Commons file page, named creator and reusable Creative Commons licence recorded in `ROUTE_IMAGE_LICENSES.md`.
7. **Timing is labelled as editorial.** Distances can come from the published route; ride times are planning estimates unless a source supplies a time. They are never represented as live traffic.


## One-stop-shop editorial lens

Rider Comms should not collapse bikers and car enthusiasts into one generic audience. The same public road can be excellent for both, but the reasons can differ. Catalogue research therefore records the following questions even when the current client does not yet display every answer:

- **Motorcycle character:** corner rhythm, cambers, exposure, crosswinds, livestock, surface changes, single-track etiquette and whether a smaller-capacity bike is a sensible match.
- **Driving character:** flow, sightlines, width, braking/acceleration transitions, surface quality, traffic density and whether the road remains enjoyable at the posted limit rather than only when driven illegally.
- **Scenic value:** views, landscape, notable stops and whether the route still has a reason to exist when traffic prevents a flowing drive.
- **Practical value:** fuel, cafés/meeting points, route length, access from population centres and whether the road works as a quick run, half-day loop or destination trip.
- **Current constraints:** road works, long-term closures, permanent speed-limit changes, average-speed enforcement, seasonal restrictions and known congestion pressure.
- **Route role:** local quick run, technical pass, flowing road, scenic tour, destination day or future multi-day itinerary.

A famous road is not automatically a good Rider Comms recommendation. The catalogue should prefer roads that remain rewarding at legal, appropriate speeds and should explicitly downgrade, annotate or defer roads whose current engineering, access or traffic situation changes the experience.

## Expansion verification — 19 September 2026

### A272 Petersfield–Petworth

The strongest publishable shape is **Petersfield → Midhurst → Petworth**, not the full A272. Rider UK gives the full Petersfield–Maresfield run as 54 miles / about 1h22 and explicitly calls the Petersfield–Cowdray stretch a highlight. Visit Midhurst independently describes Midhurst as sitting on the A272 between Petersfield and Petworth and calls the road iconic and beautiful.

Current-works screen: Hampshire's planned A272 Langrish closure is west of Petersfield and therefore outside this proposed eastbound segment. However, West Sussex's 2026–27 highways programme lists resurfacing on the A272 from Mills Farm Road to Terwick Hill near Rogate as pre-construction. That lies inside the proposed corridor, so final admission waits for a dated works check rather than pretending the road is unaffected.

### B4391 Ffestiniog–A4212

The quality case is now strong for both audiences. Supercar Driver identifies the eight-mile Ffestiniog–A4212 section as a favourite driving road and explicitly frames its appeal around flow and involvement at legal speeds. Motorcycle road sources and current rider discussion independently identify the same section as a standout North Wales ride.

The old closure blocker can now be cleared with evidence rather than assumption. Gwynedd's Llan Ffestiniog order took effect on 24 December 2025 and was limited to a maximum of three months. The later Pantllwyd order took effect on 3 March 2026 and was limited to a maximum of six months, with the works expected to take about 113 days. Both notices are now marked expired; even the longer statutory maximum ended by 3 September 2026. A fresh search of September 2026 Gwynedd public notices found no newer B4391 closure.

That is enough to stop treating a summer closure report as if it were permanent, but it is **not** equivalent to a real-time all-clear. Traffic Wales' public alert list is focused on the motorway and trunk-road network, so absence there cannot prove a local B-road is open. Before admission, do one final same-day Gwynedd/local-road check and validate the ordered geometry from Llan Ffestiniog to the A4212 junction. The route shape itself is unusually well defined: Supercar Driver gives eight miles between Ffestiniog and the A4212, while Best Biking Roads records the same Ffestiniog–Arenig corridor at 14 km.

The Commons record places the photographed road at approximately 52.9560, -3.9000 and explicitly identifies it as the B4391, so it is route photography rather than generic Snowdonia scenery.

### A32 / A339 South-East screen

The earlier shortlist combined two popular local rider roads without enough modern evidence. That is no longer a good admission path.

- **A339 Alton–Basingstoke:** enthusiast sources describe a genuinely engaging road and explicitly note it can be enjoyable within the posted limits, but they also repeatedly flag hidden entrances/junctions and a serious casualty history. Hampshire Police recorded serious collisions on the A339 in both January and April 2026. Those incidents are not evidence that the entire road is inherently unsafe, but they reinforce the need for a current authority-level route assessment before Rider Comms recommends it.
- **A32 Meon Valley:** the road remains useful as a connector around West Meon, but Hampshire Police recorded a fatal motorcycle collision near Wickham in February 2026. Current public notices also show continuing works on roads feeding into the A32/West Meon area. There is not enough upside over the A272 to justify rushing it into the catalogue.

Decision: focus South-East expansion on the better-evidenced **A272 Petersfield–Petworth** candidate first, and leave A32/A339 out until a stronger current route/safety case emerges.

### B1135 Hethel

The driving character is not the problem. PistonHeads describes the B1135 between the A11 and Hethel as a short sequence of flowing bends with varying radii and cambers, and motorcycle-route sources independently use the same Hethel corridor.

The current road state is the blocker. South Norfolk's official Hethel Roundabout and Link Road programme lists a **full B1135 Wymondham Road closure through 25 September 2026**, then temporary two-way traffic lights from 25 September to 9 October, followed by off-line completion works into late November. Norfolk County Council also made new Hethel-area 30 mph and 50 mph traffic orders on 15 September, effective **19 September 2026**.

A local parish update suggested the closure might finish on 18 September, but that conflicts with the current official project programme. Use the council programme as the safer source of truth. Decision: **defer B1135 Hethel until the junction works and new traffic layout settle**, then reassess the road as it actually exists after construction.

### Peak District / Midlands screen

I do not want the catalogue gap to force a weak admission. The **Hope Valley / Winnats / Edale loop** remains the strongest long-term Peak District candidate, but a newly published Winnats Pass closure means it is no longer a sensible near-term admission.

- **Hope Valley / Winnats / Edale loop:** Carole Nash's motorcycle route explicitly rides the A6187 from Hope through Castleton and continues up Winnats Pass. Accessible Derbyshire independently publishes a scenic-drive sequence from Castleton past Blue John/Mam Tor, over Rushup Edge to Edale, then through Hope and back to Castleton on the A6187. Visit Peak District also publishes 100% road Hope Valley routes using the A6187. Derbyshire County Council's higher-collision motorcycle-road list does not include the A6187; that is only a comparative signal, not proof that the route is low-risk.
- **Current access blocker:** Derbyshire County Council has published a temporary closure of **Winnats Pass from 1 October to 25 November 2026**, from the A6187 junction to Sheffield Road, for a new power connection. The published diversion uses A6187, Edale Road and Sheffield Road. This changes the decision: do **not** admit the loop immediately before a planned closure that breaks its defining section. Re-check after 25 November and only then complete the mileage/turn-sequence routing pass. The separate 2026 Castleton red-route changes remain stopping/parking controls, not a through-road closure.

- **A5004 Long Hill:** popular and scenic, but Derbyshire records 8 summer motorcycle collisions and 4 killed/seriously injured riders over the same period. The council also lists Fernilee culvert repair design work on the A5004. It does not get admitted merely to tick the Peak District box.

## Geometry verification notes

- **Surrey Hills Circuit:** Visit Surrey publishes the exact loop and leg lengths: Guildford → Newlands Corner (4 mi) → Shere (3 mi) → Box Hill (9.8 mi) → Leith Hill (10 mi) → Guildford (12 mi), totalling 38.8 miles. The catalogue's 39-mile loop and waypoint order match the official itinerary.
- **Norfolk B1145:** Motorcycle Sport & Leisure explicitly identifies the Bawdeswell → Gayton stretch as 17 miles. North Elmham and Litcham sit on that B1145 corridor and are retained as waypoints to prevent routing drift. Current advanced-rider reporting also flags agricultural mud/gravel as a realistic Norfolk-road hazard after wet weather.
- **A507 Hertfordshire Sweep:** Bike Stop defines the road as Baldock → Buntingford, passing Cottered with the Cumberlow Green junction on the same A507. The existing intermediate point east of Cottered is sufficient to keep routing on the intended road; no extra detour is needed.
- **Dartmoor Crossing:** Dartmoor National Park confirms the B3212 is one of the principal east–west roads across the park, and its current visitor guidance warns that ponies, sheep and cattle roam freely and asks drivers to keep below 40 mph on moorland roads. This independently supports the catalogue's livestock and speed-limit safety copy.
- **West Cornwall Coast Road:** Adventure Bike Rider gives St Ives → St Just as a 13-mile B3306 ride, and current Visit Cornwall guidance independently identifies the B3306 from St Ives west through Zennor and St Just as one of Cornwall's scenic drives. The app now uses the current official tourism page as its primary route URL while retaining the motorcycle-source corroboration here.
- **Exmoor Coast:** the route source was changed away from ABR's 2021 article because that article recommends the private toll-road fork. ABR's scenic-road coverage instead describes the public A39 from Porlock across 25% Porlock Hill to Countisbury/Lynmouth, which matches the curated line. Exmoor National Park independently confirms the A39 connects Porlock with Lynton/Lynmouth and highlights the exceptional Porlock/Countisbury gradients.
- **Llangynidr Mountain Road:** Adventure Bike Rider defines the B4560 ride as Talgarth → Beaufort, 16.9 miles, matching the 17-mile catalogue entry. A Powys County Council traffic notice schedules a temporary B4560 closure at Llangynidr from **5–13 October 2026** for ground-investigation works. Keep the route, but surface the dated closure warning and re-review it after 13 October rather than letting temporary works become permanent catalogue copy.
- **Wye Valley Sweep:** current Visit Wales guidance explicitly identifies the A466 as the valley road winding from Monmouth to Chepstow and separately recommends the A466 through Tintern to Monmouth. The existing Monmouth → Tintern → Chepstow waypoint chain therefore follows the intended corridor rather than merely connecting the two towns.
- **Hartside Pass:** Westmorland and Furness Council currently lists an A686 Hartside weather camera among its live road-condition cameras. The route remains a legitimate public-road recommendation, but its exposed summit character justifies retaining the weather/ice warning and checking current conditions before departure.
- **North Pennines B6277:** Adventure Bike Rider gives Barnard Castle → Alston as an explicit 31-mile route and describes the same remote high-road character used in the catalogue. Middleton-in-Teesdale is on the intended corridor and remains a useful anti-shortcut waypoint.
- **Honister & Newlands Circuit:** Lake District National Park currently recommends the circular drive from Keswick via Honister Pass, Newlands Pass, Buttermere and Crummock Water, and separately warns that Honister is among the UK's steepest roads and that ordinary sat-nav times can be unrealistic on narrow local roads. The catalogue's challenging classification is therefore deliberate.
- **Ribblehead & Buttertubs:** Rapid Training explicitly uses the B6255 Ribblehead → Hawes section in advanced road coaching, while current Northern DalesBus 830 runs Ribblehead → Hawes → Buttertubs Pass → Muker/Reeth on Sundays through 18 October 2026. That current scheduled service supersedes the temporary Buttertubs closure reported earlier in 2026; the road is not treated as closed, but its high-altitude/weather sensitivity remains in the safety copy.
- **Glyndŵr’s Way:** Adventure Bike Rider's route is explicitly 114 miles from Knighton through Rhayader, Llanidloes, the B4518/Clywedog mountain road, Machynlleth and A487/Cross Foxes before Lake Vyrnwy and Welshpool. The catalogue mirrors that published sequence; it remains the longest manually curated route and therefore stays under extra anti-shortcut scrutiny.
- **Duke’s Pass:** Adventure Bike Rider explicitly defines the A821 ride as Aberfoyle → Kilmahog, 14 miles, and independently includes the same Kilmahog → A821 → Aberfoyle passage in its Scotland touring-road itinerary. The Brig o’ Turk point anchors the intended A821 corridor.
- **Glencoe Run:** current VisitScotland material identifies Tyndrum as the A82 staging point for Fort William via Glen Coe and describes the road continuing over Rannoch Moor before descending into Glen Coe. The catalogue's Tyndrum → Rannoch Moor → Glencoe sequence is therefore faithful to the principal road.
- **SnowRoads:** current Visit Cairngorms guidance still defines the SnowRoads as a 90-mile route from Blairgowrie to Grantown-on-Spey via Braemar and Ballater on Britain's highest public roads. The catalogue retains 90 miles and the official endpoint/order rather than converting it into a generic Cairngorms loop.
- **North Coast A838:** motorcycle-specific sources independently identify Durness → Tongue as a 29.3-mile A838 section. The catalogue rounds this to 29 miles and uses Loch Eriboll as a route-locking waypoint.
- **Causeway Coast:** current Discover Northern Ireland guidance describes the signed coastal route between Belfast and Derry~Londonderry as roughly 75–115 miles depending on optional loops/side routes. The curated line now uses 115 miles, excludes Torr Head, and adds Portrush and Castlerock after Bushmills so external routing is less likely to cut inland before Derry.
- **Three Lochs Run:** verified against Motorcycle Scotland's published 42.9-mile / 1h14m route description. The intended order is Moffat → A708 / St Mary's Loch → Megget and Talla reservoir roads → A701 / Devil's Beef Tub → Moffat. The catalogue's 43-mile / 75-minute planning values match the published route closely. The source also confirms the reservoir leg is narrow with passing places and that there are no fuel stations on the loop.
- **Mourne Coast:** verified against the official Mourne Gullion Strangford road directions. The signed coastal element runs Newcastle → Annalong → Kilkeel → Rostrevor / Warrenpoint → Newry on the A2 corridor. The Rider Comms route uses the reverse direction, and Warrenpoint is now an explicit waypoint so external routing cannot shortcut the intended coastal section between Newry and Rostrevor.
- **Glyndŵr’s Way:** the motorcycle source confirms the 114-mile Knighton-to-Welshpool concept via Mid Wales, including the B4518 / Clywedog section, Machynlleth and the A487. Keep this route under additional waypoint scrutiny because it is the longest and most manually specified catalogue entry.

These checks are not a substitute for live routing. Before publication, every manually curated route still needs its ordered waypoint sequence reviewed for unintended motorway/trunk-road shortcuts and duplicate/backtracking behaviour.


## Routes deliberately deferred

### A57 Snake Pass

Do not add while the current engineering situation remains unresolved. Derbyshire County Council reports four landslip sites, continuing investigations and a remaining temporary 20 mph / traffic-light restriction at Alport, with long-term repair work still being developed.

Source: [Derbyshire County Council — A57 Snake Pass landslip](https://www.derbyshire.gov.uk/transport-roads/roads-traffic/landslips/snake-pass/a57-snake-pass.aspx)

### Hardknott and Wrynose Passes

These are famous, but fame is not enough for the general catalogue. Lake District National Park explicitly lists Hardknott and Wrynose among the steepest roads in the country and warns that narrow roads, tight corners and sat-nav timings can be unsuitable or unrealistic. They can be reconsidered later as clearly labelled specialist/experienced-rider routes.

Source: [Lake District National Park — sat-nav and road guidance](https://lakedistrict.gov.uk/explore/plan-your-visit/getting-to-and-around/postcodes-for-sat-nav/)

### Bealach na Bà

Keep out of the general first-party catalogue for now. Highland Council documentation describes the high route as frequently blocked by drifting snow and highlights steep gradients, hairpins and severe drops at the road edge. If added later, it should be an explicitly specialist route with live seasonal-access checking.

Source: [Highland Council winter-service material](https://www.highland.gov.uk/download/meetings/id/23688/Item7TEC6713.pdf)

### Full North Coast 500

Do not represent a 500+ mile multi-day itinerary as one ordinary route card. Rider Comms should surface the best reproducible sections (such as Durness–Tongue) until the product has proper multi-day itinerary support.

## Ongoing review rule

A route being in the catalogue is not permanent. Re-check road-authority conditions, source availability and image licensing before store releases and periodically thereafter. If long-term works materially change the ride, prefer temporarily removing or annotating the route over leaving a famous but misleading recommendation in place.
